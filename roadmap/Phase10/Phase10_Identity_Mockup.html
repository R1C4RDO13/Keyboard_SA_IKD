Phase 10 — Rebranding & New Identity

Visual mockup to lock the rebrand. Decisions already made are pinned at the top; the open question — which icon glyph — is below with five concrete options rendered against the existing 19-colour launcher palette so you can see how each reads in real conditions.

1Locked decisions

These came out of the planning conversation. Listed here for reference; not asking again.

locked App name
MoodScript
Mood + Script. Both pillars of the app in one word — emotional state (Mood) and the typing / keystroke data (Script) it's derived from. "Moodly" is already taken; this is the stronger replacement anyway.
locked Scope
Cosmetic only
Name + identity + About screen + new launcher glyph. No applicationId rename, no store/fastlane work, no ikd.db migration.
locked About screen
Local override
New org.fossify.keyboard.activities.AboutActivity centred on MoodScript with a small acknowledgement of the upstream Fossify project at the bottom.
locked Palette
Keep 19 colours
Existing Material-700 palette stays — versatile, costs nothing to keep. Icon picker (Settings → Customize Colors → App icon) untouched.
locked Launcher glyph
Single new design
One new foreground vector replaces the current keyboard-with-dots glyph. Applied across all 19 background colours via the existing adaptive-icon mechanism.
locked Constraint
White-on-colour foreground
Glyph must read cleanly on all 19 background colours, so it has to be a strong silhouette in white (or near-white). No multi-colour glyphs.

2Icon glyph — pick one

Seven candidates. Each shows a large preview, a strip of mini-tiles against eight representative background colours from the 19-colour palette, and brief pros/cons. Tip: open this file in your browser at full width; the radio buttons remember your pick on this page.

A · Soft smiley face

Direct mood-app signal · most recognisable
red · purple · blue · teal · green · yellow · orange · grey-black
A round face with two dot eyes and a soft smile arc. Reads instantly as a mood app — no learning curve, no parsing required.
ProsUniversally legible · scales to launcher pixel sizes · "I check in on my mood here" is obvious
ConsRisks looking like Daylio / How We Feel / 100 other mood apps · doesn't hint at typing/keyboard at all
option A

B · Concentric pulse rings

Abstract · mood radiating from a tap
same eight backgrounds — note how it stays calm/legible across all of them
A central dot with two soft rings expanding outward. Reads as "a tap, and something propagates" — maps to the project's whole mission: a single keystroke yields a signal about your state. Distinct from existing mood apps; works equally well for "anxiety control" and "self-knowledge" framing without painting a face on it.
ProsDistinctive · scales beautifully · respects the wellness/calm tone · ages well
ConsLess literal — first-time install needs a tagline to land · could look like a wifi or radar icon at small sizes
option B

C · "M" wordmark

Brand initial · letter-mark approach
letter-marks read at all 8 backgrounds; rounded strokes keep it soft
Letter M with rounded soft strokes, drawn as a single continuous path. Most uniquely "MoodScript" — the brand initial is the icon. No glyph illustration needed; pure typography.
ProsMost directly tied to the brand name · cheapest to design · timeless
ConsDoesn't say "mood" or "keyboard" or anything but "M" · risk of being confused for Microsoft / Medium / a thousand other M-mark apps
option C

D · Bubble + mood dots

Daily check-in framing
three dots inside a speech bubble — "how do you feel today?"
Speech-bubble outline with three dots of varying sizes inside — like the typing-indicator dots in a chat. Says "check in here" and quietly hints at the keyboard origin (the dots are also keystrokes).
ProsCarries both the chat-bubble and the keystroke-dots reading · most "warm" / human of the five
ConsMost detail of any option — risks getting muddy at small launcher sizes · could read as a generic messaging app
option D

E · Smile + pulse line

Hybrid · mood + measurement in one mark
faint smile in background, clinical pulse line in foreground
A faded smile arc behind a medical-style pulse line. Captures both halves of the mission — mood (smile) and measurement (pulse). Slightly clinical but warm; reads as "this app reads my emotional pulse from how I type."
ProsTells the whole story in one mark · most aligned with the IKD-research framing · differentiates from pure mood apps
ConsTwo overlaid layers can crowd at launcher sizes · pulse line risks reading as a fitness/heart-rate app
option E

F · Keyboard cradled by hearts

Bigger centred keyboard (G-style) · concentric heart outlines as halo
centred keyboard (slightly smaller) · three concentric hearts with breathing room
A larger version of the Fossify keyboard glyph (260×130, ~23% bigger than the original) sits centred at the icon's middle in G's outline style — stroke-only rectangle with solid white dots and spacebar inside. Three concentric complete heart outlines wrap around it like a halo, fading outward. Says "this keyboard is held by care." Both brand pillars in one mark — bigger keyboard reads at any size; hearts add the wellbeing dimension.
ProsKeyboard is the unmissable focal point · matches G's outline-style for visual consistency across options · hearts cradle, don't crowd · scales well at notification icon sizes (24dp)
ConsThree concentric outlines can read as a "wedding / valentines" icon at first glance · heart imagery is wellness-app territory · slightly busy in the centre where keyboard outline + inner heart sit close together
option F
Updated · matches original glyph

G · Tiny keyboard + pulse arcs

Original Fossify keyboard glyph at the base · pulse arcs above
same Fossify keyboard glyph as today · pulse arcs added above
The original Fossify keyboard glyph (rounded rectangle with two rows of 5 key dots and a spacebar) sits at the base, with two soft pulse arcs fanning upward. Says "the keyboard is still the keyboard — but now it broadcasts a signal." Smallest visual change from today, biggest gain in meaning.
ProsUnambiguously a keyboard · matches the existing app's iconography for continuity · arcs add the MoodScript dimension cleanly without crowding · scales well
ConsMost conservative of the seven — risk of being mistaken for "the same Fossify icon" · arcs could read as wifi to a casual glance
option G
