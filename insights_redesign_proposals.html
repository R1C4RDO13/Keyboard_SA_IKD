Insights — Redesign Proposals

Three concrete ways to thin the top menu of DashboardActivity. The current design stacks five horizontal rows before the first chart appears (KPI strip · tab toggle · range toggle · mood chip row · plus the toolbar). Below: the current state for reference, then three redesigns ranked by cost vs. payoff. Each mock is HTML/CSS — not pixel-perfect Android, but enough to compare structure.

Current — 5 chrome rows ≈ 35–40% of viewport before charts

What's painful

  • The two filter rows (range + mood) are set-once-then-ignored for most users — but they consume permanent vertical space.
  • The KPI strip carries 6 cells. On a 360 dp screen they only fit by horizontally scrolling, which is awkward when you want to compare at a glance.
  • Tab toggle + range toggle look visually identical (both are MaterialButtonToggleGroups). Two segmented controls stacked is a confusing UX hierarchy — one is navigation, the other is filter, but the eye treats them the same.
  • Mood chip row only appears after the first mood entry — but when it does, it's a fifth row.
density: heavy (5 chrome rows / charts pushed below the fold)

Tabs as bottom nav · most chart real-estate

Trade-off

  • Tabs persistently visible without competing with filter controls.
  • Title bar gains active-tab name (e.g. "Insights · Trends") so the navigation context is preserved despite the move.
  • Charts get the most vertical space of any option — useful for the histograms and heatmaps that crowded under shorter layouts.
  • Hard ceiling at 5 tabs. If a future Phase 9.x adds a sixth section, you'd have to drop one or move to bottom-sheet navigation.
  • Replaces the swipeable ViewPager2 with a NavController-driven FragmentContainerView — biggest structural change of the three options.
  • Bottom nav competes for thumb space with the system gesture bar / IME on edge-to-edge devices.

Cost

~2 days

Wins

Max chart space

Risk

Loses swipe gesture

density: light top · persistent bottom nav

Recommendation

Where to start.

Start with Option A. It directly addresses "too many buttons on the top" by removing 9 visible control buttons (4 range + up to 7 mood) and replacing them with a single toolbar icon plus one chip. It costs less than half a day and is fully reversible.

If after living with A you want to push further, layer Option B on top — A and B compose cleanly. The collapsing-KPI behaviour just adds scrollFlags to the existing AppBarLayout; A's filter chip stays in the lower (always-visible) tier of the bar.

Option C is the right call only if you decide tabs should become app-wide chrome rather than a per-screen control. It's the biggest disruption and locks tab count at 5.

Suggested rollout order: ship A first (most impact per hour). Live with it for a week. If the KPI strip still feels heavy when reading charts, layer B. Don't pre-commit to C — its trade-offs (loss of swipe gesture, 5-tab cap, biggest refactor) are only worth it if A+B together still aren't enough.
